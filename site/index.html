Schedule = fantasy points per game (PPR) that each team's remaining opponents allow to that position, from this season's box scores. Higher means easier, and the rank is out of 32. Playoffs = Weeks 15–17 only. Team factor = scoring offense, adjusted if the starting QB is out. You can override any team factor.

The model

Value = Base × Team × Schedule × Availability × (1 + News)
Base = weighted blend of Real trades, Expert consensus, and Production

1. Real trades

Redraft values from FantasyCalc, built from real trades in real leagues and pulled separately for each scoring and QB format. Rescaled so the top player in your format is 100.

2. Expert consensus

FantasyPros rest-of-season positional rankings, via DynastyProcess. An expert rank is turned into a value by looking up what the player at that same rank is worth on the trade market. So if experts rank someone RB8, he gets the market's RB8 value.

3. Production

Points per game from nflverse box scores in your scoring format, weighted 60% season-long and 40% last four games. A waiver-level player's average is subtracted (QB12, or QB24 in Superflex, RB30, WR36, TE12), and the result is scaled to match the market.

4. Team situation

Teams that score a lot lift their players, up to ±8%. If the starting QB is out or on IR, the team's pass catchers and running backs take another hit.

5. Strength of schedule

For every remaining game, how many fantasy points that opponent has allowed per game to the player's position this season (adjusted toward league average early in the year). One standard deviation easier than average is +4%. Weeks 15–17 count more if your team is playoff-bound.

6. Availability

Week by week from now through Week 17, the chance he's in your lineup. That accounts for injury status (from Sleeper, sharpened by news), games already ruled out, bye weeks, and a durability rating based on how many games he's played over the last two seasons. Your team's situation decides which weeks count most.

7. News

Headlines from RSS feeds are matched to players and scored. If AI scoring is on (free with GitHub Models), it catches indirect effects too, like a starter's injury helping his backup. Otherwise keyword rules handle the clear cases: IR, suspensions, benchings, promotions. Waiver-wire trends from Sleeper add a small bump of up to ±4%.

The trade verdict

Package values are summed. The side taking more players pays a roster-spot cost for each extra player, since someone has to be cut. The side getting the single best player gets a stud premium worth 15% of the gap. Within 5% counts as fair.
